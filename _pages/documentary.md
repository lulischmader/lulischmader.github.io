# Documentary Work

Ñañapura is a collaborative documentary project developed as part of Chapter 5 of my dissertation, “Ñañapura: Embodied Resistance to Epistemic Erasure and Extractivist Development Among Kichwa Women in the Ecuadorian Amazon.”

The documentary emerged through ethnographic fieldwork conducted in the Ecuadorian Amazon between 2023 and 2025 in collaboration with Kichwa women from the Venecia Derecha community in Alto Napo. Developed through community-based and collaborative methodologies, the project explores Indigenous women’s experiences of territorial defense, environmental activism, cultural memory, and collective care in the context of extractivist development.

The film examines how Kichwa women mobilize storytelling, music, language, community practices, and environmental knowledge to confront mining, oil extraction, and ecological degradation in the Ecuadorian Amazon. The documentary also explores ñañapura (“among sisters”) as a framework for Indigenous feminist praxis grounded in reciprocity, relationality, and collective responsibility. :contentReference[oaicite:1]{index=1}

## Current Status

The documentary is currently in post-production and collaborative editing. Because the project remains in development, the full film has not yet been publicly released or uploaded to online platforms.

A preliminary trailer and early demo version of the project were presented at the University of Miami in April 2024 as part of the lecture:

*“Ñañapura: Exploring Sisterhood and Collaboration Among Ecuadorian Kichwa Women of the Amazon and Their Struggle to Protect Their Environment.”* :contentReference[oaicite:2]{index=2}

## Documentary Themes

- Indigenous sovereignty and territorial defense  
- Environmental justice and extractivism  
- Indigenous feminisms and collective care  
- Kichwa language and cultural preservation  
- Collaborative and community-based storytelling  
- Ethnographic and audiovisual methodologies  

## Fieldwork and Collaboration

The documentary was developed collaboratively with Kichwa women activists, artists, and community members in the Ecuadorian Amazon. The project combines ethnographic research, oral history, documentary filmmaking, and audiovisual storytelling to create accessible and community-engaged forms of scholarship.
